    Respiratory illnesses explained

    Respiratory conditions affect the airways, lungs, and breathing muscles. They can range from temporary irritation and infections to chronic diseases that change how the body exchanges oxygen and carbon dioxide. Many illnesses become more noticeable when environmental triggers such as cold air, smoke, allergens, humidity extremes, or poor air quality are present.

    What to watch for

    Common warning signs include shortness of breath, wheezing, chest tightness, persistent cough, fatigue, fever, or unusual sputum production. A sudden change in symptoms, especially after allergens, smoke exposure, or a respiratory infection, should prompt closer attention.

    Symptoms Causes Prevention Treatment

    Asthma

    What it is: Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways that can cause intermittent narrowing and swelling, making breathing feel tight or limited.

    • Symptoms: wheezing, coughing, chest tightness, nighttime symptoms, shortness of breath.
    • Causes and triggers: allergens, cold air, exercise, smoke, pollution, stress, respiratory infections.
    • Risk factors: family history, allergies, low air quality exposure, premature birth, obesity.
    • Prevention: avoid triggers, use prescribed inhalers, maintain indoor air quality, and follow a care plan.
    • Treatment: controller medications, rescue inhalers, trigger avoidance, and regular follow-up with a clinician.
    • CDC Asthma
    • Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America

    Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

    What it is: COPD is a progressive disease that damages the lungs and airways, often leading to airflow obstruction and reduced exercise tolerance.

    • Symptoms: chronic cough, mucus production, wheezing, dyspnea, frequent chest infections.
    • Causes and triggers: long-term smoking, air pollution, occupational dust, recurrent lung irritation.
    • Risk factors: smoking history, older age, genetics, long-term exposure to fumes or dust.
    • Prevention: avoid smoking, reduce exposure to irritants, maintain vaccinations, and treat infections promptly.
    • Treatment: bronchodilators, inhaled steroids, pulmonary rehab, oxygen therapy in advanced cases.
    • CDC COPD
    • NHLBI COPD

    Pneumonia

    What it is: Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs, often causing fluid or pus to collect and reducing oxygen transfer.

    • Symptoms: fever, chills, productive cough, chest pain, fatigue, shortness of breath.
    • Causes and triggers: bacteria, viruses, fungi, aspiration, and immune suppression.
    • Risk factors: age, chronic disease, weakened immune defenses, hospitalization, smoking.
    • Prevention: vaccination, hand hygiene, avoiding smoking, and prompt treatment of severe infections.
    • Treatment: antibiotics when bacterial, supportive care, fluids, oxygen, and rest depending on severity.
    • CDC Pneumococcal
    • Johns Hopkins Pneumonia Education

    Bronchitis and chronic airway irritation

    What it is: Bronchitis is inflammation of the bronchial tubes, which can be acute after infection or chronic due to repeated airway irritation.

    • Symptoms: cough, mucus, throat clearing, chest discomfort, fatigue, occasional wheezing.
    • Causes and triggers: viral or bacterial infections, smoke, pollution, occupational fumes, cold exposure.
    • Risk factors: smoking, frequent infections, air pollution, asthma, postnasal drip.
    • Prevention: avoid irritants, manage allergies, stay hydrated, and seek care early for infections.
    • Treatment: rest, fluids, inhalers if needed, and antibiotics only when a bacterial cause is likely.
    • NHLBI Bronchitis
    • Mayo Clinic Bronchitis

    Sleep apnea

    What it is: Sleep apnea is a disorder in which breathing repeatedly pauses during sleep, reducing oxygen delivery and disrupting rest.

    • Symptoms: loud snoring, daytime sleepiness, morning headaches, concentration problems, witnessed pauses.
    • Causes and triggers: airway narrowing, obesity, enlarged tonsils, nasal obstruction, alcohol, and sleeping position.
    • Risk factors: obesity, older age, male sex, neck circumference, family history.
    • Prevention: maintain a healthy weight, treat nasal congestion, and avoid alcohol near bedtime.
    • Treatment: CPAP or other breathing support, positional therapy, weight loss, and surgery in some cases.
    • NHLBI Sleep Apnea
    • Sleep Foundation

    Allergic and environmental airway disease

    What it is: Allergic respiratory conditions happen when the immune system overreacts to triggers such as pollen, dust, molds, pet dander, or chemicals.

    • Symptoms: sneezing, runny nose, itchy eyes, cough, wheeze, throat irritation, congestion.
    • Causes and triggers: pollens, dust mites, mold spores, animal dander, smoke, perfumes, and household chemicals.
    • Risk factors: eczema, hay fever, family history, urban pollution, poor indoor ventilation.
    • Prevention: reduce indoor allergens, use filtration, wash bedding, and keep windows closed during high pollen.
    • Treatment: antihistamines, nasal sprays, avoidance strategies, and medical evaluation when symptoms persist.
    • CDC Allergies and Climate
    • American Academy of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology
